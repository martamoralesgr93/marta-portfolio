## CASE 01: CEF EDTECH

### What works ✅
- Spine is solid (quick summary, research, decisions, results, learnings)
- 100% WCAG AA accessibility is a real achievement
- PM Widget (work → method → decision) is sophisticated
- Visual rhythm is clean

### What tanks the level 🔴
1. **No business problem.** "Built 47 components" ≠ "Solved X bottleneck that was blocking growth"
2. **No decision narrative.** Build vs. Buy is mentioned but not explained. Why BUILD? What was the ROI?
3. **Research has no data.** 5 discovery phases (dt-grid) are decoration. Where are the findings?
4. **Metrics are weak.** WCAG AA is compliance, not impact. How many hours saved? How many teams unblocked?
5. **Learnings are generic.** "Data first, sketch second" could apply to any project.

### What to remove ✂️
- Discovery Timeline (dt-grid). 5 phases with no findings = noise.

### What to add 📝
- **Opening:** "32 screens, 8 different component approaches, design-dev feedback loops broke at handoff. Time-to-ship: 5+ weeks per feature."
- **Decision point:** "Build vs. Buy framework. Why BUILD: [ownership + velocity + cost]"
- **Hypothesis:** "If we create one unified component system + token architecture, we reduce design-to-handoff time by 40% and scale to 15 products without re-architecting."
- **Real implementation:** 3 actual product screens using the system. Proof it works.
- **Quantified results:** 
  - Handoff time: 6 weeks → 3 weeks
  - Product coverage: 5 → 15 products
  - Consistency: 32 different → 1 standard
- **Trade-off:** "Chose token-based over component-library because [reason]. Lost [flexibility] to gain [scalability]."
- **Learnings:** "Assumed inconsistency was design problem. Was actually architecture problem."

### Reorder to:
1. Hero
2. Quick Summary
3. **Problem (business):** Fragmentation blockedgrowth
4. **Decision:** Build vs. Buy
5. **Hypothesis:** What you expected
6. Research (real findings, not phases)
7. **Design decisions:** Why this architecture?
8. Implementation (real screens)
9. Results
10. Learnings

### What to write immediately:
- 3-sentence paragraph: "The problem was..."
- 1-sentence explanation of BUILD decision
- 1 learning that surprised you

### Metrics to highlight:
- NOT: "100% WCAG AA" (compliance)
- YES: "Unified 32 inconsistent approaches into 1 system. Designers ship 40% faster."

### How to increase seniority perception:
- Show the trade-off you made (not just the win)
- Name the constraint you worked within (AEM? Legacy code? Stakeholder requirements?)
- Quantify secondary impact ("Beyond speed, consistency reduced QA time by 30%")

## CASE 02: ILUNION CRO

### What works ✅
- Hero metric is visceral: 3.36% → 6.5%
- Audit composition pattern (evidence + findings) is clean
- Experiment card (hypothesis → decision → result) is clear
- Funnel visualization shows drop-off

### What tanks the level 🔴
1. **Findings without why.** "47% drop at step 2" — but WHAT happens at step 2? Why do they leave? You show the stat, not the story.
2. **Before/After is UI-only.** Why that specific layout? What problem does it solve? No reasoning.
3. **No user research.** "Data reveals friction" — but did you talk to users? Watch them? Clarity heatmaps show WHERE, but not WHY.
4. **Playtime is hidden.** "3.36% → 6.5%" over what period? 1 week? 6 months? Without time, the metric is meaningless.
5. **Quick Summary is vague.** Role = "CRO Lead" but you're a designer. Role = "Product Designer" is honest. Timeframe missing.
6. **No constraint narrative.** You designed within AEM. AEM is rigid. How did you work within that?
7. **Learnings are hollow.** "Data first" — OK, but what specifically did you learn from THIS project?

### What to remove ✂️
- Overview 2x2 (ov-card). Replace with 1 strong paragraph on the brief.

### What to add 📝
- **Opening:** "Direct channel booking had a 3.36% conversion rate. Where were users dropping? Clarity + analytics showed 47% abandoned at step 2 [THE OFFER]. Why? User research revealed: [specific reason]."
- **User research method:** "5 user interviews + Clarity session recordings showed users were confused by [X specific thing about the offer]."
- **Hypothesis:** "If I simplify the offer presentation + reduce decision points, conversion rises to 5%+"
- **Design decision + reasoning:** "Changed layout from [A] to [B] because [user research finding]. Tested hypothesis: [result]."
- **Constraint narrative:** "Designed within AEM's HTML limitations. Couldn't use custom JS, so solved with CSS + [technique]."
- **Playtime:** "Launched Aug 2024. Measured over 8 weeks, 12,500 users, 95% confidence."
- **Secondary metrics:** "Conversion +94%. Also: AOV +12%, bounce rate -18%, form errors -35%."
- **Trade-off:** "Chose simplicity over personalization because data showed that choice paralysis was the blocker."
- **Learnings:** "Assumed users understood offer mechanics. User research showed they didn't read. Redesigned with assumption of 10-second comprehension."

### Reorder to:
1. Hero
2. Quick Summary (include timeframe)
3. **Problem:** User research + data (where/why drop-off)
4. **Hypothesis:** What you expected
5. **Decision:** Why this design change?
6. Wireframes (with reasoning, not just shape)
7. Before/After UI
8. Implementation (AEM constraint narrative)
9. Results (conversion + secondary metrics)
10. Learnings (what surprised you)

### What to write immediately:
- 1 sentence: "Users abandoned because..."
- 1 sentence: "I tested by..."
- 1 sentence: "Result was surprising because..."

### Metrics to highlight:
- 3.36% → 6.5% over [X weeks] with [X users], confidence [X]%
- AOV, bounce, errors (secondary)
- NOT: Raw conversion % without time/sample/confidence

### How to increase seniority perception:
- Name the user research method (not just "data shows")
- Specify the trade-off you chose
- Show that you measured secondary impacts (holistic thinking)
- Explain the constraint you worked within

### What tanks the level 🔴

2. **No hypothesis validation.** "If I create strong identity, what happens?" You built it, but did it move the needle?
3. **Design system is a catalog.** Paleta → Typo → Components. But WHY each choice? No reasoning, no constraints acknowledged.
4. **"2026 Lab" section is random.** What is it? Future roadmap? IA experiment? It seems disconnected.
5. **No SMB constraints visible.** WordPress, budget, DIY workflow — the real story. But you don't mention them.
6. **Growth attribution missing.** 5 locales in 2 years. How much was design-driven? Honest answer increases credibility.

### What to remove ✂️
- "2026 Lab" OR explain it clearly (if it's an experiment, name the learning; if it's roadmap, move it; if it's unrelated, delete it)

### What to add 📝
- **Opening:** "Paolo is a 1-location artisanal pizzeria. No visual identity. Random photos on socials. Competing with every other pizza place in Madrid. The owner wanted to scale to 5 locations. Without strong brand, that was risky."
- **Problem:** "Visibility problem: looked like every other pizzeria. No differentiation on social or web."
- **Hypothesis:** "If I create a distinctive visual identity + consistent ecosystem, we attract more customers and support growth to 5 locations."
- **Design decisions + reasoning:**
  - Color choice: "Warm orange + cream because [personality + competitive landscape analysis]"
  - Typography: "[Serif] + [sans] because [legibility + character]"
  - Tone: "[Casual, warm] because [SMB personality]"
- **Constraint narrative:** "Paolo's constraint: WordPress-only, DIY design updates, monthly budget $X. Designed system that non-designers could maintain."
- **Ecosystem documentation:** Show web + Instagram + email side-by-side. Annotate what repeats (colors, typography, tone). COHERENCE is the win.
- **Growth story:** "Scaled from 1 → 5 locales over 24 months. Design-driven growth? Attribution: organic (word-of-mouth) 60%, social + web presence 40%. Strong brand made social + web 2x more effective."

- **Learnings:** "Designing for SMB isn't downscale enterprise design. It's designing for personality + constraint + maintainability."

1. Hero

5. **Design decisions:** Why color? Why type? Why tone?
6. **Constraints (SMB + WordPress):** How you solved within limits
7. **Ecosystem:** Web + socials + email side-by-side (coherence proof)
8. **Scaling:** 1 → 5 locales (system holds)
9. Results (locations, social, orders, recognition)

## CASE 05: IT OPS & ORACLE ERP

### What works ✅
- Internal tooling is differentiator (few designers show this)
- Spine is present
- HTML without images but well-visualized shows technical chops

### What tanks the level 🔴
1. **Too technical, not enough empathy.** "Built tool using Figma + Oracle" — but WHY? What was the ops pain?
2. **Title is descriptor, not narrative.** "Product Research & Internal Tooling" reads like a job description, not a case.
3. **No research evidence.** Title says research, but I don't see method or findings.
4. **Constraint narrative invisible.** Oracle ERP is famously rigid. How did you design within that?
5. **Impact is soft.** Conversions in Case 2 are hard metrics. Ops satisfaction is soft. You need something quantifiable.
6. **Adoption story missing.** Does anyone use this? How many? How often?
7. **Power user understanding unclear.** Are ops engineers the users? What makes them tick? What's their workflow?

### What to remove ✂️
- Purely technical diagrams without narrative context. Every diagram should answer "So what?"

### What to add 📝
- **Opening:** "IT Ops team spends 30+ minutes per request validating forms, managing state transitions, debugging. Error rate: 15%. Frustration: high. I was asked to redesign their workflow."
- **Research method:** "Shadowed 5 ops engineers. Conducted interviews. Ran Clarity on Oracle native interface. Key finding: [specific bottleneck]."
- **Hypothesis:** "If I create a custom validation + state management layer, ops can complete requests 60% faster with 80% fewer errors."
- **Design decisions:** "Built for power users (not mastery learners). Keyboard shortcuts, progressive disclosure, error prevention instead of error recovery."
- **Constraint narrative:** "Oracle API is rigid. Can't alter core ERP. Designed a custom layer on top that integrates via API calls. Trade-off: [what you lost to gain speed]."
- **Workflow before/after:** 
  - Before: 30 min, 3 form validations, 2 manual state checks, high error rate
  - After: 10 min, 1-click validation, automatic state, <2% errors
- **Adoption:** "Live with 15 ops engineers. Usage: 200 requests/week. Satisfaction: 4.8/5 NPS."
- **Results:**
  - Time per request: 30 min → 10 min (67% reduction)
  - Error rate: 15% → 2% (87% reduction)
  - Team adoption: 100%
  - NPS: 4.8/5
- **Learnings:** "Power users value efficiency over hand-holding. Shortcuts + context > wizards."

### Reorder to:
1. Hero
2. Quick Summary
3. **Problem:** Ops pain (time, errors, frustration)
4. **Research:** Method + findings
5. **Hypothesis:** What you expected
6. **Design decisions:** Why this approach for power users?
7. Workflow before/after (visual)
8. Technical diagrams (with narrative: "Here's how this solves X")
9. Implementation details
10. Results (time, errors, adoption, satisfaction)
11. Learnings

### What to write immediately:
- 1 sentence: "Ops engineers were spending X because [specific bottleneck]"
- 1 sentence: "Research revealed [key finding]"
- 1 sentence: "The Oracle constraint was [what you did]"

### Metrics to highlight:
- Time per request: 30 → 10 minutes (quantified impact)
- Error rate: 15% → 2% (quality improvement)
- Adoption: 15/15 ops using (proof of value)
- NPS: 4.8/5 (satisfaction)
- NOT: "Built internal tool" (feature, not impact)

### How to increase seniority perception:
- Show power user understanding (not all users are the same)
- Quantify hard metrics (time, errors, adoption)
- Name the constraint you worked within (Oracle rigidity)
- Be honest about trade-offs (what you couldn't do)

## CROSS-CASE PATTERNS TO FIX

| Problem | Fix | Impact |
|---------|-----|--------|
| **No business problem** | Every case opens with user/business friction (not your solution) | Shifts narrative from "I built" to "I solved" |
| **Hypothesis invisible** | Write it: "If I change X, expect Y because Z" | Shows strategic thinking, not just execution |
| **Constraint hidden** | Name it: "AEM limited me to..." or "Budget constrained to..." | Proves real-world experience + creativity under pressure |
| **Metric without time** | Add: "Over [weeks], with [N users], confidence [%]" | Credibility (not made-up numbers) |
| **Generic learnings** | Specific: "Assumed X. Wrong. Now know Y" | Shows reflection + growth mindset |
| **Trade-off omitted** | "Chose A over B because data showed..." | Demonstrates judgment (not just execution) |
